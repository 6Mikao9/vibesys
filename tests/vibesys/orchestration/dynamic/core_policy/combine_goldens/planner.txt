## Performance bounds

A bound is an estimate of a fixed limit, never a prediction of the current code.
Name the scored metric and the unit of work. Name the resource each unit must
consume: bytes moved, operations, round trips, or a serialized dependency.
Divide that work by the hardware rate. State the result as a range, and state
each assumption with the kind of evidence behind it.

Keep two limits separate. The hardware limit is what this computation can reach
on this hardware for the fixed workload, independent of the current design. The
design ceiling is what the current design can reach. It may sit far below the
hardware limit, and that gap is a reason to change the design, not a reason to
lower the hardware limit.

Write the bounds before any measurement exists. Revise them when understanding
improves: a measured hardware rate against the spec sheet, the real shape of the
workload, a byte count taken from the code, a measurement that beats the bound
(then the bound is wrong), or a plateau far below the bound (check the bound,
then the code). Never derive a bound from a candidate's own speed. A revision
needs independent evidence: a microbenchmark, a byte count from the code, or a
profile. Hardware rates come from the platform notes for this run's backend.

Report every measurement as a fraction of its bound. Rank proposed work by the
time that could still be recovered between the measurement and the bound.
